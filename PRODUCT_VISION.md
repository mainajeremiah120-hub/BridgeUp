# BridgeUp Product Vision

## Product Direction

BridgeUp is an interest-first global social platform.

People join BridgeUp because of what they care about, what they want to learn,
what they can teach, what they are building, and who they want to meet. Location,
university, city, country, and organization are useful context, but they are not
the primary structure of the product.

The old model was:

```text
Country -> City -> University -> Department -> Interest
```

The new model is:

```text
Interest -> Communities -> Discussions -> Live Rooms -> People -> Events
```

Country, city, university, school, and organization should become optional
filters inside discovery, not the foundation of the platform.

## Positioning

BridgeUp helps people around the world find communities, conversations, mentors,
collaborators, events, and friends through shared interests, skills, goals,
languages, and professions.

Short positioning:

```text
Find your people by interest, skill, and ambition.
```

Expanded positioning:

```text
BridgeUp is a global social platform for communities, live rooms, discussions,
mentors, events, and collaborators around what you care about.
```

## Product Personality

BridgeUp should feel:

- Alive
- Global
- Curious
- Social
- Useful
- Welcoming
- Fast to explore
- Serious enough for growth, but casual enough for friendship

BridgeUp should not feel like:

- A university directory
- A location hierarchy
- A school management system
- A static list of institutions
- A professional network with no play or spontaneity

## Inspiration Mix

BridgeUp can borrow useful patterns from:

- Reddit: topic-based discussions and voting
- Discord: communities, channels, live rooms, and presence
- LinkedIn: profile credibility, professions, skills, mentorship
- Meetup: events, groups, and local discovery
- OmeTV: spontaneous global conversation and live social energy

BridgeUp should remain unique by combining interest discovery, live rooms,
skill exchange, mentorship, and meaningful global profiles in one place.

## Core Discovery Model

Users should be discoverable through:

- Interests
- Skills
- Goals
- Languages
- Professions
- Communities
- Events attended
- Live rooms hosted or joined
- Mentorship availability
- Optional country, city, university, school, or organization

Primary discovery questions:

- Who is interested in what I care about?
- Who can help me learn this?
- Who wants to learn what I can teach?
- Who is building something similar?
- Which rooms are live now?
- Which conversations are popular today?
- Which events are happening now or soon?
- Which communities are active in my interests?
- Who speaks my language?
- Who is near me, if I care about location?

## Core Entities

### Interests

Interests are the top-level entry point into BridgeUp.

Examples:

- Programming
- Cybersecurity
- AI
- Psychology
- Fitness
- Business
- Startups
- Christianity
- Languages
- Gaming
- Music
- Photography
- Design

Interest pages should show:

- Top communities
- Popular discussions
- Live rooms
- Mentors
- Top contributors
- Events
- New people to follow
- Related interests

### Communities

Communities are groups around a shared interest, goal, profession, identity,
project, event, or location.

Examples:

- AI Builders
- Cybersecurity Beginners
- Startup Founders Circle
- Christian Creatives
- Psychology Students and Practitioners
- French-English Language Exchange
- UI/UX Design Lab
- Fitness Accountability Club
- Nairobi Startups
- Multimedia University Developers

Location-based and university-based communities are allowed, but they should be
one type of community, not the default product structure.

### Discussions

Discussions are public or community-based posts.

Examples:

- What should I learn after Python?
- How do you stay consistent at the gym?
- Best beginner cybersecurity labs?
- Is AI replacing junior designers?
- Looking for accountability partners for IELTS.

Discussion cards should show:

- Title
- Interest tags
- Community
- Replies
- Votes or reactions
- People active now
- Language
- Optional location context

### Live Rooms

Live rooms create the feeling that BridgeUp is alive right now.

Room types:

- Voice rooms
- Video rooms
- Text rooms
- Study rooms
- Debate rooms
- Practice rooms
- Mentorship office hours
- Event rooms
- Random interest-matched social rooms

Examples:

- AI Builders Room
- English Practice: Beginner Friendly
- Startup Pitch Feedback
- Christian Young Adults Hangout
- Cybersecurity CTF Help Desk
- Design Portfolio Reviews

### People

Profiles should represent the person as a whole, not just their school.

Important profile fields:

- Name
- Avatar
- Headline
- Bio
- Interests
- Skills I have
- Skills I want to learn
- Goals
- Languages
- Profession or path
- Communities
- Mentorship topics
- Online status
- Reputation
- Country and city, optional
- University, school, and organization, optional

### Mentors

Mentorship should be a first-class discovery surface.

Mentor cards should show:

- Topic
- Availability
- Languages
- Reputation
- Response time
- Communities active in
- Free or paid status in the future

Examples:

- React mentor online now
- Startup pitch feedback available
- Fitness coach answering questions
- Cybersecurity mentor reviewing portfolios

### Events

Events should connect global interests with real-time activity.

Examples:

- Build your first API workshop
- English speaking practice
- 30-day fitness accountability challenge
- Remote work vs office work debate
- Bible study room
- Startup pitch night
- Photography critique session

## Onboarding

Signup should not begin with location as the main identity.

Recommended onboarding flow:

1. Pick interests
2. Choose goals
3. Add skills
4. Add languages
5. Add profession or path
6. Add optional location, university, organization
7. Join recommended communities
8. Follow recommended people
9. Enter a live room or answer a discussion prompt

## Migration From Current Concept

Current product concepts that can stay:

- Communities
- Channels
- Chat
- Profiles
- Skills
- Mentorship
- Reputation
- Location fields
- University fields
- Organization fields

Current product concepts to change:

- `Directory Explorer` should become `Discover`.
- `Community Trees` should become interest/community discovery.
- The homepage hierarchy example should be removed.
- Country/city/university/department should become optional filters.
- Signup should prioritize interests, goals, skills, and languages.
- Community levels should not hard-code country, city, university, major, and
  interest as the only hierarchy.

## Success Criteria

BridgeUp is successfully pivoted when:

- A new user can join without being tied to a school.
- The homepage immediately shows global activity.
- Interests are the main entry point.
- Live rooms and discussions make the platform feel active.
- People can discover each other by interests, skills, goals, languages, and
  professions.
- Location and university are useful filters, not the product foundation.
- The product feels relevant to users from any country.
